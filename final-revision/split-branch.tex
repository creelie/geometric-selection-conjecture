\section{The split branch on the tetraquadric}\label{sec:splitbranch}
The ledger of the next section records two failures at the fixed datum
that do not depend on the complex structure: the residual Abelian factor
of Section~\ref{sec:extraU1} and the Yukawa deficiency of
Proposition~\ref{prop:rankfailure}. This section asks whether another
choice of five line bundles on the same manifold could avoid them while
staying at the split locus. The first failure cannot be avoided by any
sum of line bundles on any Calabi--Yau threefold with at most four K\"ahler
parameters; the second is a property of the charge matrix, and the
selection rules decide it in finitely many steps for each datum.

\subsection{The residual Abelian factor is forced}\label{sec:forced}
Let $X$ be a Calabi--Yau threefold, $h=h^{1,1}(X)$, and let $J_1,\dots,J_h$
be divisor classes spanning $H^{1,1}(X,\Q)$. A sum of line bundles
$V=\bigoplus_{a=1}^nL_a$ with $c_1(L_a)=\sum_ik_a^iJ_i$ has the integer
charge matrix $K=(k_a^i)\in\Z^{h\times n}$, whose columns are the vectors
$k_a$. For a K\"ahler class $J$ write $\kappa_i(J)=\int_XJ_iJ^2$.

\begin{theorem}[The residual factor]\label{thm:forced}
Suppose $c_1(V)=0$ and $V$ is slope-polystable with respect to $J$. Then
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item $\rk K\le h-1$;
\item the traceless kernel $\{q\in\Q^n:\ Kq=0,\ \sum_aq_a=0\}$ has dimension
at least $n-h$;
\item at the split locus, with vanishing expectation values of all charged
singlets, exactly $n-1-\rk K\ge n-h$ of the $n-1$ traceless Abelian gauge
bosons of the structure group $S(U(1)^n)$ are massless.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, for $n=5$ and $h\le4$ at least one Abelian gauge boson
beyond the Standard Model stays massless at every split point of every
rank-five sum of line bundles on $X$, and condition C3, read as the
visible gauge group being exactly the Standard Model group, fails there.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The slope of $L_a$ is $\mu_J(L_a)=\int_Xc_1(L_a)J^2=\sum_ik_a^i\kappa_i$.
A polystable sum has all summands of slope $\mu_J(V)$, and
$\mu_J(V)=\int_Xc_1(V)J^2=0$. Thus $K^{T}\kappa=0$. The vector $\kappa$ is
nonzero, because $\sum_it_i\kappa_i=\int_XJ^3>0$ when $J=\sum_it_iJ_i$.
Hence the rows of $K$ satisfy one nontrivial linear relation and
$\rk K\le h-1$, which is (i). Since $c_1(V)=\sum_ac_1(L_a)=0$, the vector
$\one=(1,\dots,1)$ lies in $\ker K$, which has dimension
$n-\rk K\ge n-h+1$; the hyperplane $\sum_aq_a=0$ does not contain $\one$,
so it meets $\ker K$ in a space of dimension at least $n-h$, which is (ii).

For (iii) we use the Green--Schwarz masses of the Abelian gauge bosons as
computed by Anderson, Gray, Lukas and Palti \cite[Section~2.7]{AGLP12}.
The axions of the $h$ K\"ahler moduli and the universal axion shift under
the $U(1)$ gauge transformations with coefficients $k_a^i$ and
$\beta_ik_a^i$ respectively, and the mass matrix of the gauge bosons
$A_a$ in the basis of the $n$ summands is
$M_{ab}=k_a^{T}\widetilde Gk_b$, where
$\widetilde G=G+\beta\beta^{T}/(4s^2)$, $G$ is the positive definite
metric on the K\"ahler moduli and $s$ the dilaton. The matrix
$\widetilde G$ is positive definite, so the quadratic form
$q\mapsto q^{T}Mq=(Kq)^{T}\widetilde G(Kq)$ vanishes exactly on $\ker K$:
the combination $\sum_aq_aA_a$ is massless if and only if $Kq=0$. On the
traceless vectors, which parametrize the gauge bosons of $S(U(1)^n)$, the
massless combinations form the space of (ii), of dimension
$n-1-\rk K$. This is the count of \cite[eq.~(2.31)]{AGLP12}, and (i) gives
the lower bound $n-h$, which is the statement of \cite[eq.~(2.32)]{AGLP12}
that fewer than five K\"ahler moduli leave at least one $U(1)$ massless.
A nonzero traceless $q$ in $\ker K$ acts nontrivially on some summand, so
the surviving gauge boson is not a redundant generator.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Reading the fixed datum]\label{rem:forcedfixed}
For the matrix \eqref{eq:bundle}, $\rk K=3=h-1$, the largest value
Theorem~\ref{thm:forced} allows, and $\ker K$ is spanned by $\one$ and the
vector $q$ of \eqref{eq:extrau1}. The residual factor of
Section~\ref{sec:extraU1} is therefore not a defect of the choice
\eqref{eq:bundle}; it is the minimum that the tetraquadric, with
$h^{1,1}=4$, imposes on every rank-five split datum. The polarization
locus is tied to the same number. The constraint $K^{T}\kappa=0$ confines
$\kappa$ to a space of dimension $h-\rk K=1$, the line through
$(1,1,1,1)$, and for $J=\sum_it_iJ_i$ with all $t_i>0$ the intersection
numbers of Proposition~\ref{prop:cover} give
$\kappa_i=4\,e_2(t_j:j\ne i)=4\bigl(e_2(t)-t_i(e_1(t)-t_i)\bigr)$, where
$e_1,e_2$ are the elementary symmetric functions. All four $\kappa_i$ are
equal exactly when $t_i(e_1(t)-t_i)$ takes one value, so each $t_i$ is a
root of $x(e_1-x)=c$, hence $t_i\in\{c',e_1-c'\}$ for one $c'$; if $m$ of
the coordinates equal $c'$ and $4-m$ equal $e_1-c'$, summing gives
$(m-3)e_1=(2m-4)c'$, which has a solution with all $t_i>0$ only for $m=0$
or $m=4$, that is, all coordinates equal. Thus the ray $J=\lambda\sum_iJ_i$
of Section~\ref{sec:pointfailure} is the whole locus of K\"ahler classes
at which \eqref{eq:bundle} is polystable, and the flat classical direction
of C7 and the massless generator of C3 are two readings of the one
equality $4-\rk K=1$. For $n=h+1$ the two counts $n-1-\rk K$ and
$h-\rk K$ agree for every split datum.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Selection rules on eleven split data}\label{sec:eleven}
Let $X\subset(\PP^1)^4$ be any smooth tetraquadric and $V=\bigoplus_{a=1}^5
\cO_X(k_a)$ with $c_1(V)=0$. Under the structure group $S(U(1)^5)$ the
charged fields carry the charges of Section~\ref{sec:extraU1}, in the
coordinates $e_1,\dots,e_5$ modulo the diagonal $\one$, as listed in
Table~\ref{tab:charges}.

A term of the perturbative superpotential is a product of charged fields
times a monomial in the singlets, and it is gauge invariant exactly when
its total charge is a multiple of $\one$. For the down-type coupling
$\mathbf{10}_a\,\overline{\mathbf5}_{bc}\,\overline{\mathbf5}_{de}$ the charge
$Q=e_a+e_b+e_c+e_d+e_e$ has coordinate sum five, so invariance after
insertion of singlets requires $\one-Q$ to be a nonnegative integer
combination of the available singlet charges; for the up-type coupling
$\mathbf{10}_a\mathbf{10}_b\mathbf 5_{de}$ the same is required of
$e_d+e_e-e_a-e_b$. Which groups are nonzero depends on $X$, but on every
smooth tetraquadric the restriction sequence
$0\to\cO_A(k-2\one)\to\cO_A(k)\to\cO_X(k)\to0$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:koszulbound}
 h^i(X,\cO_X(k))\le h^i(A,\cO_A(k))+h^{i+1}(A,\cO_A(k-2\one)),
\end{equation}
with the ambient groups given by the K\"unneth formula on $A=(\PP^1)^4$.
We call a field \emph{possibly present} when the bound
\eqref{eq:koszulbound} for its group is positive. A coupling forbidden
with every possibly present singlet is forbidden on every smooth
tetraquadric in the class; a coupling allowed by the charges is only not
excluded by them.

\begin{lemma}[Flow criterion]\label{lem:flow}
Let $E$ be a set of ordered pairs $(p,q)$ of distinct elements of
$N=\{1,\dots,n\}$ and $v\in\Z^n$ with $\sum_pv_p=0$. The following are
equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item $v=\sum_{(p,q)\in E}m_{pq}(e_p-e_q)$ with integers $m_{pq}\ge0$.
\item For every $B\subset N$ that no pair of $E$ leaves, that is, $p\in B$
and $(p,q)\in E$ imply $q\in B$, one has $\sum_{p\in B}v_p\le0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A pair $(p,q)$ contributes $e_p-e_q$, whose sum over $B$ is $1$ if
$p\in B\not\ni q$, $-1$ if $q\in B\not\ni p$ and $0$ otherwise. When no pair
leaves $B$ the first case does not occur, so (a) gives
$\sum_Bv\le0$, which is (b).

Conversely, form the network with a source $s$, a sink $t$, the vertices
of $N$, arcs $s\to p$ of capacity $v_p$ for $v_p>0$, arcs $q\to t$ of
capacity $-v_q$ for $v_q<0$, and arcs $p\to q$ of unbounded capacity for
$(p,q)\in E$. Let $S\ni s$, $T\ni t$ be a cut of finite capacity and
$B=S\cap N$. No pair of $E$ leaves $B$, since such an arc would cross the
cut with unbounded capacity. The capacity of the cut is
\[
 \sum_{p\notin B,\ v_p>0}v_p+\sum_{q\in B,\ v_q<0}(-v_q)
 =\sum_{v_p>0}v_p-\sum_{p\in B}v_p\ \ge\ \sum_{v_p>0}v_p
\]
by (b). The cut $S=\{s\}$ has capacity $\sum_{v_p>0}v_p$, so this is the
minimum, and the maximal flow theorem of Ford and Fulkerson \cite{FF56}
gives an integral flow of that value, because all capacities are
integers. It saturates every arc at $s$ and at $t$. Let $m_{pq}$ be the
flow on the arc $p\to q$. Conservation at $p\in N$ says that
$\sum_qm_{pq}-\sum_qm_{qp}$ equals the inflow from $s$ minus the outflow
to $t$, which is $v_p$. This is (a).
\end{proof}

The criterion is decided by inspecting the $2^5$ subsets; the cases below
were also checked by a maximal flow computation.\footnote{The program
\texttt{code/yukawa\_charges.py} of the repository named in the note under
the abstract lists, for each datum, the possibly present fields by
\eqref{eq:koszulbound}, decides the two couplings by a maximal flow and
prints either a closed set $B$ violating (b) or an explicit decomposition
(a). Its output is the file \texttt{yukawa\_charges\_results.json}.}

\begin{proposition}[Eleven split data]\label{prop:eleven}
Consider the matrix \eqref{eq:bundle} and the ten rank-five sums of line
bundles with entries in $[-2,2]$ listed in Table~\ref{tab:eleven}. Each
of them has $c_1(V)=0$, the index $\chi(X,V)=-3|G|$ for the stated order
$|G|$, the anomaly condition $\int_Xc_2(V)J_i\le24=\int_Xc_2(T_X)J_i$ for
every $i$, $H^2(X,V)=0$ on every smooth tetraquadric by
\eqref{eq:koszulbound}, and a K\"ahler class at which all five slopes
vanish, at an explicit algebraic point.\footnote{These properties are
checked in exact arithmetic by \texttt{code/gamma\_branch.py}; the points
are $t=(1,1,1,1)$ for \eqref{eq:bundle} and the classes $6$, $8$, the points
of Table~\ref{tab:twoblocks} for the classes $2$, $7$ and $10$, the point of
the class $10$ also for the class $9$, and
$\bigl(1,\tfrac{\sqrt7-1}3,\tfrac{\sqrt7-1}3,1\bigr)$, $(1,\tfrac52,1,1)$,
$(1,1,\tfrac14,1)$, $(4,4,4,1)$ for the classes $1$, $3$, $4$, $5$. The
ten sums were found by a computer search, and the list is not claimed to
contain every sum with these properties.} With all possibly present singlets
inserted to every order:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item for \eqref{eq:bundle} every down-type coupling vanishes on every
smooth tetraquadric, while the up-type couplings
$\mathbf{10}_2\mathbf{10}_5\mathbf 5_{25}$ and, with one singlet of charge
$e_5-e_2$, $\mathbf{10}_2\mathbf{10}_2\mathbf 5_{25}$ and
$\mathbf{10}_5\mathbf{10}_5\mathbf 5_{25}$ are allowed by the charges;
\item for the classes $9$ and $10$ every up-type and every down-type
coupling vanishes; for the classes $3$, $4$ and $5$ every up-type coupling
vanishes;
\item for the classes $1$, $2$, $6$, $7$ and $8$ both couplings are
allowed by the charges, with the singlet insertions of
Table~\ref{tab:eleven}.
\end{enumerate}
Hence six of the eleven split data fail C8 to all orders of the
perturbative singlet expansion on every smooth tetraquadric, and the five
others are not excluded by the selection rules.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each datum the possibly present fields follow from
\eqref{eq:koszulbound} and the K\"unneth formula
$h^0(\PP^1,\cO(m))=m+1$ for $m\ge0$, $h^1(\PP^1,\cO(m))=-m-1$ for $m\le-2$,
all other groups zero. For \eqref{eq:bundle} the possibly present
$\mathbf{10}$'s lie in $H^1(L_2)$ and $H^1(L_5)$, the
$\overline{\mathbf5}$'s in $H^1$ of $L_1L_2$, $L_2L_4$, $L_2L_5$, $L_4L_5$,
the $\mathbf5$'s in $H^2$ of $L_1L_2$, $L_2L_5$, and the singlets have
charges $e_p-e_q$ with $p\in\{2,4,5\}$ and $(p,q)$ one of
$(2,1),(2,3),(2,4),(2,5),(4,2),(5,1),(5,2),(5,3)$. No singlet has $p=1$ or
$p=3$, so the sets $B=\{1\}$ and $B=\{3\}$ are left by no pair. For a
down-type coupling $v=\one-Q$ has $v_3=1-Q_3=1$, since no charged field
carries the coordinate $e_3$; Lemma~\ref{lem:flow}(b) fails at $B=\{3\}$
and the coupling is forbidden. This recovers
Proposition~\ref{prop:singletobstruction} for every smooth tetraquadric
with the matrix \eqref{eq:bundle}. For the up-type couplings
$Q=e_a+e_b-e_2-e_5$ with $a,b\in\{2,5\}$; $Q=0$ for $\{a,b\}=\{2,5\}$, and
for $a=b=2$ the singlet $(5,2)$ gives $e_5-e_2=-Q$, for $a=b=5$ the singlet
$(2,5)$. This is (i).

For (ii) and (iii) the same two steps are carried out for each row of
Table~\ref{tab:eleven}. Where the table gives a set $B$, the reader
checks that no possibly present singlet $(p,q)$ has $p\in B$, $q\notin B$,
and that $\sum_Bv>0$ for every choice of the charged fields: for the
classes $9$ and $10$ the summand $L_1$ carries no $\mathbf{10}$ and lies in
no possibly present $\overline{\mathbf5}$, so $v_1=1$ for every down-type
coupling, while for the up-type couplings $B$ contains the coordinates of
every possibly present $\mathbf 5$ and no $\mathbf{10}$, so
$\sum_Bv=2>0$; for the classes $3$, $4$, $5$ the set $B$ contains a
coordinate of every possibly present $\mathbf5$ and misses the
coordinates of the $\mathbf{10}$'s. Where the table gives a coupling with
insertions, the listed singlets are possibly present and their charges
add to $\one-Q$, respectively $-Q$, which is (a) of the lemma. For the
classes $3$, $4$ and $5$ the down-type coupling listed has $Q=\one$ and
needs no insertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the proposition does and does not say]\label{rem:elevenscope}
The proposition decides a necessary condition. For the six excluded data
the conclusion is unconditional within the perturbative singlet
expansion: no complex structure, no polarization and no singlet
expectation values restore the missing matrices, since the bound
\eqref{eq:koszulbound} holds on every smooth member and
Lemma~\ref{lem:flow}(b) is violated. For the five remaining data nothing
is asserted beyond the absence of this obstruction. Their down-type
couplings arise only at second or third order in the singlets, the
singlet groups may vanish on a given member, the free action and its
projection of the spectrum have not been computed, and the stability of
the bundle after the singlets acquire expectation values, which is the
same extension problem as in Section~\ref{sec:pointfailure}, is open.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:forced} every one of the eleven data keeps a massless
Abelian gauge boson at its split point, so the five classes are candidates
for the extension branch, not for the split locus. The next subsection
decides, for each of the eleven data, whether the smallest non-Abelian
deformations of the split bundle remove that gauge boson.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The extension branch}\label{sec:extbranch}
Throughout this subsection $X\subset(\PP^1)^4$ is a smooth tetraquadric,
$V=\bigoplus_{a=1}^5L_a$ is one of the eleven data of
Table~\ref{tab:eleven} other than the class $3$, so that $\rk K=3$, and
$u\in\Z^5$ is a generator of the traceless kernel
$\{q:Kq=0,\ \sum_aq_a=0\}$, which has dimension one by
Theorem~\ref{thm:forced}(ii); the vectors $u$ are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:twoblocks}. For a K\"ahler class $J=\sum_it_iJ_i$,
$t_i>0$, we write $\mu_a(J)=\mu_J(L_a)=\sum_ik_a^i\kappa_i(J)$ and
$\mu(J)=(\mu_1,\dots,\mu_5)=K\kappa(J)$. The vector $\mu(J)$ lies in the
column space of $K$, which has dimension three and is contained in the
hyperplane $\sum_a\mu_a=0$.

The bundles that replace $V$ when the charged singlets of
Section~\ref{sec:pointfailure} acquire expectation values are the
polystable bundles $E=\bigoplus_jE_j$ of slope zero whose stable summands
$E_j$ are iterated extensions of the line bundles $L_a$, $a\in B_j$, for a
partition $\{B_j\}$ of $\{1,\dots,5\}$; they have the Chern character of
$V$, and we call the partition the \emph{shape} of $E$. The split bundle
has the shape with five blocks. The dictionary between such bundles and the
four-dimensional theory is the one of the stability walls of Anderson,
Gray, Lukas and Ovrut \cite{AGLO09,AGL09}: an extension class in
$H^1(L_pL_q^{-1})=\operatorname{Ext}^1(L_q,L_p)$ is the expectation value
of a singlet of charge $e_p-e_q$, the slopes of the summands are the
D-terms of the Abelian factors, and a non-split extension exists as a
polystable bundle exactly where the subbundle has the smaller slope.

\begin{lemma}[The Abelian factor on a branch]\label{lem:branchfactor}
Let $E=\bigoplus_{j=1}^mE_j$ be polystable of slope zero with the shape
$\{B_j\}$. The structure group of $E$ reduces to
$S\bigl(\prod_jU(|B_j|)\bigr)$, whose centre is parametrized by the
traceless vectors $q\in\Q^5$ constant on every block. The Abelian gauge
boson of such a generator $q$ is massless at $E$ if and only if $Kq=0$.
Consequently $E$ keeps a massless Abelian gauge boson beyond the
$SU(5)$ factor exactly when $u$ is constant on every block of its shape.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each $E_j$ is a holomorphic bundle of rank $|B_j|$ with a Hermitian
Yang--Mills connection, so the connection on $E$ has holonomy in
$\prod_jU(|B_j|)$, and in the subgroup with determinant one because
$c_1(E)=c_1(V)=0$ and the connection is traceless. The centre of this group
consists of the diagonal matrices with one scalar per block, that is the
vectors $q$ constant on the blocks, traceless for the determinant
condition. The Green--Schwarz masses of these generators are computed as in
the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:forced}: the axions shift under the generator
$q$ by the first Chern class $\sum_jq_{B_j}c_1(E_j)=\sum_aq_ac_1(L_a)$,
whose coordinates are $Kq$, because the first Chern class of an iterated
extension is the sum of those of its quotients. Hence the mass of $q$ is
$(Kq)^T\widetilde G(Kq)$ with $\widetilde G$ positive definite
\cite[Section~2.7]{AGLP12}, and $q$ is massless exactly when $Kq=0$. The
traceless solutions of $Kq=0$ are the multiples of $u$, so a massless
generator exists if and only if $u$ itself is constant on the blocks.
The same conclusion follows from the Higgs mechanism of
\cite{AGL09}: the extension class of a block is a singlet of charge
$e_p-e_q$ with $p,q$ in that block, and its expectation value gives a
mass to every Abelian generator $q$ with $q_p\ne q_q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[One side of a wall]\label{lem:wall}
Let $Y$ be a smooth projective threefold, $L,L^\prime$ line bundles on $Y$
and $J_0$ an ample class with $\mu_{J_0}(L)=\mu_{J_0}(L^\prime)$. Let
$0\to L\to E\to L^\prime\to0$ be a non-split extension. There is a
neighbourhood $U$ of $J_0$ in the ample cone such that $E$ is
$\mu_J$-stable for every $J\in U$ with $\mu_J(L)<\mu_J(L^\prime)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A saturated subsheaf $M\subset E$ of rank one is reflexive, hence a line
bundle, as $Y$ is smooth \cite[Section~1.1]{HL10}; it suffices to
show $\mu_J(M)<\mu_J(E)=\frac12(\mu_J(L)+\mu_J(L^\prime))$ for every
such $M$. If the composite $M\to L^\prime$ vanishes, then $M\subset L$,
so $M=L(-D)$ with $D$ effective and $\mu_J(M)\le\mu_J(L)<\mu_J(E)$.
Otherwise $M\to L^\prime$ is injective, $M=L^\prime(-D)$ with $D$
effective, and $D\ne0$, since $D=0$ would split the extension. Put
$\delta_J=\frac12(\mu_J(L^\prime)-\mu_J(L))$, so that
$\mu_J(M)<\mu_J(E)$ means $J^2\cdot D>\delta_J$.

Choose a rational ample class $H$ with $J_0-H$ ample. By Grothendieck's
lemma \cite[Lemma~1.7.9]{HL10} the saturated subsheaves $M\subset E$ with
$\mu_H(M)\ge\mu_H(L^\prime)-1$, that is with $H^2\cdot D\le1$, form a
bounded family, so only finitely many classes $D_1,\dots,D_m\in H^2(Y,\Z)$
occur among them. Each $D_i$ is effective and nonzero, so
$J_0^2\cdot D_i>0$, and $c_1=\min_iJ_0^2\cdot D_i>0$. Let $U$ be a
neighbourhood of $J_0$ in the ample cone on which $J-H$ is ample,
$J^2\cdot D_i>c_1/2$ for all $i$ and $\delta_J<\min(c_1/2,1)$; it exists
by continuity, since $\delta_{J_0}=0$. For $J\in U$ and $M$ in the bounded
family, $J^2\cdot D>c_1/2>\delta_J$. For $M$ outside it, $H^2\cdot D>1$,
and writing $J=H+(J-H)$ with both terms ample gives
$J^2\cdot D\ge H^2\cdot D>1>\delta_J$, because
$H\cdot(J-H)\cdot D$ and $(J-H)^2\cdot D$ are nonnegative on the
effective divisor $D$. In both cases $\mu_J(M)<\mu_J(E)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[One block does not suffice]\label{prop:oneblock}
No polystable bundle of slope zero whose shape has exactly one block of
size two, $E=E_1\oplus L_r\oplus L_s\oplus L_o$ with $E_1$ a non-split
extension of $L_q$ by $L_p$, removes the massless generator $u$: either no
such bundle is polystable for any K\"ahler class, or $u_p=u_q$ and $u$
stays massless by Lemma~\ref{lem:branchfactor}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Polystability of slope zero requires $\mu_r=\mu_s=\mu_o=0$, that is
$\kappa(J)\perp k_r,k_s,k_o$. If these three rows are linearly
independent, the solutions form the line $\{K^T\kappa=0\}$, since that
line has dimension $4-\rk K=1$ and is contained in them; then
$\mu_p=\mu_q=0$ as well, and $E_1$ has the subbundle $L_p$ of the same
slope, so it is not stable, and the shape does not occur. If the three rows
are dependent, some nonzero $v\in\ker K$ vanishes at $p$ and $q$. Now
$\ker K=\Q\one\oplus\Q u$, so $v=\alpha\one+\beta u$ with
$\alpha+\beta u_p=\alpha+\beta u_q=0$ and $\beta\ne0$, whence $u_p=u_q$.
\end{proof}

Two blocks of size two can do what one cannot. For a shape
$\{p,q\},\{r,s\},\{o\}$ let
\[
 W=\{\mu\in\operatorname{Im}K:\ \mu_o=0,\ \mu_p+\mu_q=0\},
\]
the slopes allowed to a polystable bundle of that shape (the third
condition $\mu_r+\mu_s=0$ follows from $\sum_a\mu_a=0$). When $W$ is a
line $\Q w$, the slopes at every admissible $J$ are $\mu(J)=f(J)w$ for a
scalar $f(J)\ne0$, so the sign of $f$ decides which member of each pair
is the subbundle: the one with $fw_a<0$. We normalise $w$ so that $f>0$
gives the orientation shown in the tables, and we say that a shape is
\emph{realised} at $J$ when the two extension groups
$H^1(L_{\rm sub}L_{\rm quot}^{-1})$ of that orientation are nonzero.

\begin{lemma}[A vanishing on the general tetraquadric]\label{lem:genericvanishing}
For the class $1$ of Table~\ref{tab:eleven}, $h^1(X,L_2L_5^{-1})=
h^1(X,L_5L_2^{-1})=0$ for every tetraquadric $X$ in a nonempty Zariski
open set of smooth members, in particular for the general one.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Here $k_2-k_5=(-3,-2,2,3)$ and $k_5-k_2=(3,2,-2,-3)$. For $k=(-3,-2,2,3)$
the K\"unneth formula gives $h^1(A,\cO_A(k))=0$ and
$h^2(A,\cO_A(k))=h^2(A,\cO_A(k-2\one))=2\cdot1\cdot3\cdot4=24$, both
groups being $H^1\otimes H^1\otimes H^0\otimes H^0$ in the four factors, so
the restriction sequence gives $h^1(X,\cO_X(k))=\dim\ker\bigl(s\colon
H^2(A,\cO_A(k-2\one))\to H^2(A,\cO_A(k))\bigr)$, where $s$ is the
equation of $X$. In the \v{C}ech description $H^1(\PP^1,\cO(m))$ has the
basis of the monomials $x^ay^b$ with $a,b\le-1$, $a+b=m$, and
$H^0(\PP^1,\cO(m))$ that of the monomials with $a,b\ge0$; a polynomial
acts by multiplication, and a product whose exponents leave the range is
zero. For the quadric
\begin{multline*}
 s_0=x_1^2x_2^2y_3^2x_4^2+y_1^2y_2^2x_3^2x_4^2+y_1^2x_2^2x_3^2y_4^2
 +x_1^2y_2^2y_3^2y_4^2\\+y_1^2(x_2y_2)(x_3y_3)y_4^2+x_1^2(x_2y_2)(x_3y_3)x_4^2
\end{multline*}
each of the $24$ monomials of the source is carried by exactly one of the
six terms to a nonzero monomial of the target, and the $24$ images are
distinct, so the map is a bijection of the two bases; the same holds for
$k=(3,2,-2,-3)$ with the roles of the factors exchanged. Injectivity of a
linear map is an open condition on $s$, and the smooth members form a
nonempty open set, so both hold on a nonempty open set.\footnote{The
program \texttt{code/extension\_branch.py} of the repository verifies the
two permutation matrices and lists, for every datum and every shape with
two blocks of size two, the space $W$, the orientations and the exact
extension dimensions used below; its output is the file
\texttt{extension\_branch\_results.json}.}
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Two blocks]\label{thm:twoblocks}
Let $V$ be one of the eleven data of Table~\ref{tab:eleven} other than the
class $3$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item For the classes $4$, $5$ and $9$ there is no K\"ahler class $J$ and no
polystable bundle of slope zero of a shape with two blocks of size two on
any smooth tetraquadric. For the class $1$ the same holds on every
tetraquadric of Lemma~\ref{lem:genericvanishing}.
\item For the datum \eqref{eq:bundle} and the classes $2$, $6$, $7$, $8$
and $10$ there are a shape, listed in Table~\ref{tab:twoblocks}, and a
K\"ahler class $J_0$ at which $V$ is polystable, such that the locus
$S=\{J:\mu_o(J)=0,\ \mu_p(J)+\mu_q(J)=0\}$ is a smooth surface near
$J_0$ on which $\mu=fw$ with $f$ changing sign along every curve through
$J_0$ transverse to $\{f=0\}$, and such that for every $J\in S$ with
$f(J)>0$ close enough to $J_0$ and every pair of nonzero classes in the two
extension groups of the table, which are nonzero on every smooth
tetraquadric, the bundle $E_1\oplus E_2\oplus L_o$ is polystable of
slope zero, has the Chern character of $V$, and has structure group
$S(U(2)\times U(2)\times U(1))$ with no massless Abelian gauge boson
beyond $SU(5)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) A polystable bundle of slope zero and shape $\{p,q\},\{r,s\},\{o\}$
has $\mu(J)\in W$. For each of the four data, Table~\ref{tab:excluded}
lists the shapes with $W\ne0$, and in every one of them $W$ is a line; in
the other shapes all five slopes vanish and the extensions have a
subbundle of equal slope, so they are not stable. On a listed shape
$\mu(J)=fw$ with $f\ne0$, and for each sign of $f$ the subbundle of a
block is its member of negative slope. The table lists for each sign a
group $H^1(L_{\rm sub}L_{\rm quot}^{-1})$ that vanishes, on every smooth
tetraquadric when its bound \eqref{eq:koszulbound} is zero, which is
checked with the K\"unneth formula as in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:eleven}, and on the tetraquadrics of
Lemma~\ref{lem:genericvanishing} in the four cases marked with an
asterisk, which are the groups $H^1(L_2L_5^{-1})$ and $H^1(L_5L_2^{-1})$
of the class $1$ and their copies with $L_3\cong L_2$. The corresponding
block has no non-split extension, so the shape does not occur.

(ii) Fix a row of Table~\ref{tab:twoblocks} and write
$\{p,q\}=\{{\rm sub}_1,{\rm quot}_1\}$, $\{r,s\}$ for the second block.
The slopes vanish at $J_0$: for $t=(1,1,1,1)$ every $\kappa_i$ equals $12$
and every row of $K$ has coordinate sum zero; for the other three $J_0$ the
five polynomials $\mu_a(t)$ vanish at the listed algebraic point, which is
verified by exact arithmetic. The surface $S$ is smooth at $J_0$ when the
differentials of $\mu_o$ and $\mu_p+\mu_q$ are independent there. For
$i\ne j$ one has $\partial\kappa_i/\partial t_j=4(e_1(t)-t_i-t_j)$ and
$\partial\kappa_i/\partial t_i=0$, so at $t=(1,1,1,1)$, using the zero
coordinate sum of the rows, $d\mu_a=-8\sum_jk_a^j\,dt_j$: the two
differentials are $-8$ times the rows $k_o$ and $k_p+k_q$, which are
independent for the three symmetric data (for \eqref{eq:bundle},
$(1,1,-1,-1)$ and $(-2,-3,2,3)$). On $S$ the slopes lie in $W=\Q w$, so
$\mu=fw$ with $f=\mu_{q}/w_q$; its differential along $S$ at $J_0$ is
nonzero, for \eqref{eq:bundle} because $k_1$ is not in the span of $k_4$
and $k_1+k_2$, and for the other data by the same exact computation. Hence
$f$ takes both signs on $S$ arbitrarily close to $J_0$.

For $J\in S$ with $f(J)>0$ the slopes of the subbundles are
$fw_{\rm sub}<0$ and those of the quotients $fw_{\rm quot}>0$, while
$\mu_o=0$ and $\mu_{\rm sub}+\mu_{\rm quot}=0$ on each block. The two
extension groups are nonzero on every smooth tetraquadric, because for each
the bound \eqref{eq:koszulbound} is attained: the K\"unneth groups
$H^1(A,\cO_A(k-2\one))$ and $H^2(A,\cO_A(k))$ vanish, so the restriction
sequence gives $h^1(X,\cO_X(k))=h^1(A,\cO_A(k))+h^2(A,\cO_A(k-2\one))$,
which is the number in the table. Lemma~\ref{lem:wall}, applied to each
block at $J_0$, gives a neighbourhood $U$ of $J_0$ on which both $E_1$ and
$E_2$ are stable for every $J$ with $f(J)>0$; their slopes and that of
$L_o$ are zero, so $E=E_1\oplus E_2\oplus L_o$ is polystable of slope zero
for $J\in S\cap U$, $f(J)>0$. Its Chern character is that of $V$. The
determinants $c_1(E_1)=c_1(L_p)+c_1(L_q)$ and $c_1(E_2)$ differ in every
row, so the two blocks are not isomorphic and the structure group is
$S(U(2)\times U(2)\times U(1))$. Finally $u$ is not constant on the
blocks: in every row $u_{\rm sub}\ne u_{\rm quot}$ for both blocks, as
Table~\ref{tab:twoblocks} shows, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:branchfactor} no
Abelian gauge boson beyond $SU(5)$ is massless at $E$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the branch gives on the cover]\label{rem:extscope}
Theorem~\ref{thm:twoblocks}(ii) removes the obstruction of
Theorem~\ref{thm:forced} for six of the eleven data, including the fixed
datum, at the cost of leaving the split locus: the polystable K\"ahler
classes of $E$ form a half of the surface $S$, of dimension two, in place
of the ray of Remark~\ref{rem:forcedfixed}, and the extension classes are
further moduli. The theorem is stated on the cover $X$. The next
subsection descends the branch to $X_\pi/\Gamma$, computes its charged
spectrum for the fixed datum and bounds its Yukawa matrices for every
datum that admits the group $\Gamma$. Condition C7 is not addressed there
either, since $S$ and the extension classes are flat directions of the
D-terms, and shapes with a block of size three or more, which by
Lemma~\ref{lem:branchfactor} remove $u$ exactly when $u$ is not constant
on the large block, are not treated. Proposition~\ref{prop:oneblock} and
part (i) close the smallest shapes for the classes $1$, $4$, $5$ and $9$;
for these the Abelian factor can only disappear on a shape with a block of
size at least three.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The branch on the quotient}\label{sec:gammabranch}
We now return to the fixed threefold $X=X_\pi$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedquotient}, with the free group $\Gamma=\langle
a,b\rangle\cong(\Z/2)^2$ and the quotient $Y=X/\Gamma$. On $\cO_X(k)$ the
untwisted lifts of $a$ and $b$ satisfy $ab=(-1)^{\sum_ik_i}ba$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:cover}), and two lifts differ by scalars, so
$\cO_X(k)$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant exactly when $\sum_ik_i$ is even, and
its equivariant structures are the untwisted one twisted by the four
characters $\chi$ of $\Gamma$. A three-family quotient by $\Gamma$ needs
the index $-12$, which singles out the data of Table~\ref{tab:eleven} with
$|G|=4$: the datum \eqref{eq:bundle} and the classes $2$, $6$, $7$, $8$, $9$
and $10$. Every row has even sum for \eqref{eq:bundle} and the classes $6$,
$8$, $9$, $10$; the classes $2$ and $7$ have a row with odd sum, so one of
their line bundles carries no $\Gamma$-action, and the class $9$ has no
bundle with two blocks of size two by Theorem~\ref{thm:twoblocks}(i). We
call the datum \eqref{eq:bundle} and the classes $6$, $8$ and $10$ the
\emph{$\Gamma$-data}.

For a $\Gamma$-datum and a shape with blocks $\{s_1,q_1\}$, $\{s_2,q_2\}$,
$\{o\}$, oriented so that $L_{s_i}$ is the subbundle of the $i$-th block, a
\emph{$\Gamma$-bundle of that shape} is $E=E_1\oplus E_2\oplus L_o$, where
the line bundles carry equivariant structures with characters
$\chi_1,\dots,\chi_5$ of product $\mathbf 1$, and $E_i$ is the extension
$0\to L_{s_i}\to E_i\to L_{q_i}\to0$ of a nonzero invariant class
$e_i\in H^1(L_{s_i}L_{q_i}^{-1})^\Gamma$. Then $E$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant
with trivial equivariant determinant and descends to a bundle $E_Y$ on $Y$
with $c_1=0$. A Wilson line $\rho\colon\Gamma\to\{1,D\}$,
$D=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,-1,-1)$, $\rho\ne1$, determines the nontrivial
character $\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon(g)=-1$ exactly when $\rho(g)=D$;
as in Section~\ref{sec:fixedbundle}, the quark doublets $Q$ lie in
$H^1(E)^\varepsilon$, the $u^c$ and $e^c$ in $H^1(E)^{\mathbf1}$, the
$d^c$ in $H^1(\Lambda^2E)^{\mathbf1}$, the lepton and down-type Higgs
doublets in $H^1(\Lambda^2E)^\varepsilon$, and the up-type Higgs doublets
$H_u$ in $H^1(\Lambda^3E)^\varepsilon$, the Serre dual of
$H^2(\Lambda^2E)^\varepsilon$. The Yukawa couplings at large volume are the
cup products followed by the wedge product into
$H^3(X,\Lambda^5E)=H^3(X,\cO_X)\cong\C$, $(x,y,h)\mapsto x\cup y\cup h$,
with $x\in H^1(E)^\varepsilon$, $y\in H^1(E)^{\mathbf1}$,
$h\in H^1(\Lambda^3E)^\varepsilon$ for the up-type matrix and
$x\in H^1(E)^\varepsilon$, $y\in H^1(\Lambda^2E)^{\mathbf1}$,
$h\in H^1(\Lambda^2E)^\varepsilon$ for the down-type matrix; the
charged-lepton matrix $Y_e$ is the same product on
$H^1(E)^{\mathbf1}\times H^1(\Lambda^2E)^\varepsilon\times
H^1(\Lambda^2E)^\varepsilon$. On $Y$ these are the restrictions to the
invariant parts, up to the factor $|\Gamma|$. For a Higgs class $h$ the
matrix $Y_u(\cdot,\cdot;h)$ is the bilinear form it defines, and its rank
is the rank of the up-type Yukawa matrix in any bases; the same holds for
$Y_d$ and $Y_e$.

\begin{lemma}[Regular representations]\label{lem:regular}
Let $F$ be a $\Gamma$-equivariant vector bundle on $X$ whose cohomology is
concentrated in one degree $q$. Then $H^q(X,F)$ is the sum of
$(-1)^q\chi(X,F)/4$ copies of the regular representation $R_\Gamma$. In
particular every character occurs in $H^1(L_a)$ with the same multiplicity
$n_a=-\chi(L_a)/4$, whatever the equivariant structure on $L_a$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $g\ne1$ the automorphism $g$ has no fixed points, and the holomorphic
Lefschetz formula~\cite{AB68} gives $\sum_p(-1)^p\operatorname{tr}(g\,|\,
H^p(X,F))=0$, so the character of $H^q(X,F)$ vanishes off the identity.
A representation of a finite group with this property is a multiple of the
regular representation.
\end{proof}

The Yukawa couplings of a $\Gamma$-bundle obey two selection rules. Let
$T=(\C^*)^3$ act on $E$ by the scalars $t_1,t_2,t_3$ on $E_1$, $E_2$ and
$L_o$. These are automorphisms of $E$ commuting with $\Gamma$, so $T$ acts
on every $H^p(\Lambda^iE)^\chi$, and we write $w(a)\in\Z^3$ for the weight
of the block containing $a$: $w(s_i)=w(q_i)=\ee_i$, $w(o)=\ee_3$. For
$I\subset\{1,\dots,5\}$ put $L_I=\bigotimes_{a\in I}L_a$ and
$w(I)=\sum_{a\in I}w(a)$. Call $S\subset\{1,\dots,5\}$ \emph{closed} when
$q_i\in S$ implies $s_i\in S$, and let $E_S\subset E$ be the subbundle
spanned by $L_{s_i}$ when only $s_i$ lies in $S$, by $E_i$ when both members
of the block do, and by $L_o$ when $o\in S$; it has rank $|S|$, and
$\Lambda^iE$ has the $T$- and $\Gamma$-stable filtration
$F^m\Lambda^iE=\operatorname{Im}(\Lambda^mE_S\otimes\Lambda^{i-m}E)$.
We say that a class of $H^p(\Lambda^iE)$ has \emph{level} at least $m$ for
$S$ when it lies in the image of $H^p(F^m\Lambda^iE)$.

\begin{lemma}[Selection rules]\label{lem:selection}
Let $x,y,z$ be classes in $H^{p_1}(\Lambda^{i_1}E)$, $H^{p_2}(\Lambda^{i_2}E)$,
$H^{p_3}(\Lambda^{i_3}E)$ with $i_1+i_2+i_3=5$ and $p_1+p_2+p_3=3$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item If $x,y,z$ are $T$-weight vectors whose weights do not add up to
$(2,2,1)$, then $x\cup y\cup z=0$.
\item If, for a closed set $S$, the levels of $x,y,z$ are at least
$l_1,l_2,l_3$ with $l_1+l_2+l_3>|S|$, then $x\cup y\cup z=0$.
\item The bundle $F^m\Lambda^iE/F^{m+1}\Lambda^iE$ has a filtration whose
quotients are the $L_I$ with $|I|=i$ and $|I\cap S|=m$, of weight $w(I)$.
Hence the $T$-weight $w$ part of $H^p(\Lambda^iE)$ vanishes unless
$H^p(L_I)\ne0$ for some $I$ with $|I|=i$ and $w(I)=w$, and if
$H^p(L_I)=0$ for every $I$ with $|I|=i$ and $|I\cap S|<m$, then every class
of $H^p(\Lambda^iE)$ has level at least $m$ for $S$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(a) The product is a $T$-equivariant map into $H^3(\Lambda^5E)$, on which
$t$ acts through $\det t=t_1^2t_2^2t_3$, of weight $(2,2,1)$; a weight
vector of another weight maps to zero. (b) The wedge product maps
$F^{l_1}\otimes F^{l_2}\otimes F^{l_3}$ into $F^{l_1+l_2+l_3}\Lambda^5E$,
which is zero since $\Lambda^lE_S=0$ for $l>|S|$; the product of the images
of classes of the three bundles is the image of their product. (c) The
quotient is $\Lambda^mE_S\otimes\Lambda^{i-m}(E/E_S)$, and $E_S$, $E/E_S$
are filtered by the sub-line-bundles $L_{s_i}$ with quotients the
$L_a$, $a\in S$, respectively $a\notin S$. The long exact sequences of
these filtrations give the first assertion, and those of
$0\to F^{m'+1}\to F^{m'}\to F^{m'}/F^{m'+1}\to0$ for $m'<m$ show that
$H^p(F^m\Lambda^iE)\to H^p(\Lambda^iE)$ is onto under the stated vanishing.
\end{proof}

By (c) and Lemma~\ref{lem:regular}, the weight $\ee_j$ part of
$H^1(E)^\chi$ has dimension at most $\sum_{a}n_a$ over the summands $a$ of
the $j$-th block, for every character $\chi$, and the up-type Higgs
classes have weights $(2,2,1)-w(\{a,b\})$ over the pairs with
$H^2(L_aL_b)\ne0$. By (a), $Y_u(x,y;h)$ can be nonzero for weight vectors
$x,y$ of weights $\alpha,\beta$ only if $\alpha+\beta=w(\{a,b\})$ for such
a pair.

\begin{theorem}[Yukawa matrices on the quotient]\label{thm:gammayukawa}
Let $E_Y$ be the descent of a $\Gamma$-bundle $E$ of one of the shapes of
Table~\ref{tab:gammashapes}, with any equivariant structures and any
invariant extension classes, and let $\rho$ be any Wilson line as above.
For every Higgs class the large-volume Yukawa matrices satisfy
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item for the datum \eqref{eq:bundle}: $\rk Y_u\le2$ and $Y_d=Y_e=0$;
\item for the class $6$: $\rk Y_u\le1$, and $Y_u=0$ except on the shape
$L_2\to L_5$, $L_4\to L_3$, $L_1$;
\item for the class $8$: $\rk Y_u\le2$;
\item for the class $10$: $Y_u=Y_d=Y_e=0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Throughout, a summand $L_a$ with $n_a=0$ is acyclic (Table~\ref{tab:pieces}),
so the weight $\ee_j$ part of $H^1(E)$ is the image of $H^1$ of the members
of the $j$-th block with $n_a>0$; if that member is the subbundle
$L_{s_j}$, these classes have level at least $1$ for $S=\{s_j\}$.

(i) Here $n_2=2$, $n_5=1$, and $L_2L_5$ is the only product with $H^2\ne0$.
In each of the three shapes $L_2$ and $L_5$ are the subbundles of the two
blocks, so $H^1(E)^\chi$ is the sum of the weight $w(2)$ part, of dimension
at most two, and the weight $w(5)\ne w(2)$ part, of dimension at most one.
The only allowed weights are $\alpha+\beta=w(2)+w(5)$, so in bases of weight
vectors $Y_u=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&A\\B&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with
$A$ of size at most $2\times1$ and $B$ at most $1\times2$, and
$\rk Y_u\le\rk A+\rk B\le2$. For the down-type and lepton matrices take
$S=\{2,4,5\}$, which is closed in the three shapes, since $L_4$ is a
quotient only of $L_2$. By Table~\ref{tab:pieces} the bundles $L_1$, $L_3$
and every $L_aL_b$ with $\{a,b\}\cap\{1,3\}\ne\varnothing$ are acyclic, so by
Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}(c) every class of $H^1(E)$ has level at least $1$
and every class of $H^1(\Lambda^2E)$ level at least $2$. The levels of the
three classes in $Y_d$ or $Y_e$ add up to at least $5>|S|=3$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}(b) gives $Y_d=Y_e=0$. In words, the cohomology of
$E$ and $\Lambda^2E$ comes from the subbundle $E_S$ of rank three, whose
fifth exterior power vanishes.

(ii) Here $n_3=n_4=n_5=1$, and $L_2L_5$ is the only product with $H^2\ne0$;
the up-type Higgs classes have weight $(2,2,1)-w(2)-w(5)$ and lie in
$H^1$ of the piece $L_1L_3L_4$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}(c) they have
level at least $1$ for $S=\{3\}$ whenever this set is closed.
For the shape $L_2\to L_5$, $L_4\to L_3$, $L_1$ the weight $w(2)+w(5)=2\ee_1$
is reached only by two classes of weight $\ee_1$, which come from $L_5$, at
most one in each character, so $\rk Y_u\le1$. For
$L_3\to L_2$, $L_5\to L_4$, $L_1$ the allowed pairs have one class of
weight $\ee_1$, from the subbundle $L_3$; with $S=\{3\}$ its level is at
least $1$, that of $h$ at least $1$, and $1+0+1>1$. For
$L_4\to L_1$, $L_3\to L_2$, $L_5$ the allowed weights are $\ee_2+\ee_3$, and
the class of weight $\ee_2$ comes from the subbundle $L_3$; the same
argument with $S=\{3\}$ applies. For $L_4\to L_1$, $L_5\to L_2$, $L_3$ and
$L_3\to L_1$, $L_5\to L_2$, $L_4$ the allowed weight is $2\ee_2$, and both
classes come from the subbundle $L_5$; with $S=\{5\}$ their levels add up to
$2>1$. Hence $Y_u=0$ on these four shapes.

(iii) Here $n_3=n_4=n_5=1$, and the products with $H^2\ne0$ are $L_2L_4$,
$L_2L_5$ and $L_4L_5$, none of which contains $L_3$. If $L_3$ is the lone
summand, the classes of weight $\ee_3$ come from $L_3$, and $\ee_3+\beta$
has third coordinate at least one while every allowed weight $w(\{a,b\})$
has third coordinate zero; so these classes pair with nothing. If $L_3$ is
the subbundle of a block, as in the first two shapes, take $S=\{3\}$: the
classes from $L_3$ have level at least $1$, and the Higgs classes lie in
$H^1$ of the complements $L_1L_3L_5$, $L_1L_3L_4$, $L_1L_2L_3$, all
containing $L_3$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}(c) they have level at least
$1$ and the classes from $L_3$ again pair with nothing. In both cases
$Y_u$ is supported on the classes from $L_4$ and $L_5$, at most two in each
character, and $\rk Y_u\le2$.

(iv) Here $n_5=3$ and the other $n_a$ vanish, and $L_2L_5$ is the only product
with $H^2\ne0$. In both shapes $L_2$ and $L_5$ lie in different blocks, so
$w(2)\ne w(5)$, while every class of $H^1(E)$ has weight $w(5)$ and
$2w(5)\ne w(2)+w(5)$; hence $Y_u=0$. For $Y_d$ and $Y_e$ take
$S=\{1,2,4,5\}$, closed in both shapes because $L_3$ is the lone summand
or a quotient. The bundle $L_3$ and its four products are acyclic, so the
classes of $H^1(E)$ and $H^1(\Lambda^2E)$ have levels at least $1$ and $2$,
and $1+2+2>4$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[C8 on the $\Gamma$-branch]\label{cor:gammac8}
Let $E$ be a $\Gamma$-bundle with two blocks of size two built from one of
the eleven data of Table~\ref{tab:eleven}, polystable of slope zero for some
K\"ahler class, whose descent to $X_\pi/\Gamma$ has three net families. For
every equivariant structure, extension classes, Wilson line and Higgs
class its large-volume Yukawa couplings violate C8$_W$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Three net families on the quotient need index $-12$ on the cover, so the
datum is one of the seven with $|G|=4$. The classes $2$ and $7$ have a line
bundle without a $\Gamma$-action, so they carry no $\Gamma$-bundle, and the
class $9$ has no polystable bundle with two blocks of size two by
Theorem~\ref{thm:twoblocks}(i). For the four $\Gamma$-data a polystable
bundle has one of the orientations allowed by the slopes and nonzero
extension classes, so its shape is in Table~\ref{tab:gammashapes}, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:gammayukawa} gives $\rk Y_u\le2$, while C8$_W$ requires
rank three.
\end{proof}

For the fixed datum the branch can be followed further: on its realised
shape it keeps the spectrum of Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedcohom} and removes
the gauge boson of Section~\ref{sec:extraU1} on the quotient itself.

\begin{theorem}[The fixed datum on the branch]\label{thm:fixedbranch}
Let $V$ be the datum \eqref{eq:bundle} on $X_\pi$ with the untwisted
structures, let $E=E_1\oplus E_2\oplus L_4$ with
$0\to L_2\to E_1\to L_1\to0$ and $0\to L_5\to E_2\to L_3\to0$, and let
$J\in S$ be a K\"ahler class of Theorem~\ref{thm:twoblocks}(ii) for this
shape.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item The invariant parts of $H^1(L_2L_1^{-1})$ and $H^1(L_5L_3^{-1})$ have
dimensions $3$ and $1$. For nonzero invariant classes $E$ is
$\Gamma$-equivariant, and its descent $E_Y$ is polystable of slope zero
with respect to the class induced by $J$ on $Y$, has the Chern character
of $V_Y$, and leaves no massless Abelian gauge boson beyond $SU(5)$.
\item There are $\Gamma$-equivariant isomorphisms $H^\ast(E)\cong
H^\ast(V)$ and $H^\ast(\Lambda^2E)\cong H^\ast(\Lambda^2V)$, so with the
Wilson line of Section~\ref{sec:fixedbundle} the charged spectrum on $Y$ is
that of Section~\ref{sec:fixedbundle}: three families, one Higgs doublet
pair, no Higgs triplets and no further charged matter.
\item $Y_d=Y_e=0$ and $\rk Y_u\le2$ for every choice of the extension classes.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) The multidegrees are $k_2-k_1=(0,-3,2,1)$ and $k_5-k_3=(1,0,1,-2)$.
In both the source $\cO_A(k-2\one)$ of the restriction sequence has a
coordinate $-1$ and is acyclic, and the target has only $H^1$, of
dimensions $1\cdot2\cdot3\cdot2=12$ and $2\cdot1\cdot2\cdot1=4$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:regular} the two groups are $3R_\Gamma$ and $R_\Gamma$, with
invariant parts of dimensions $3$ and $1$. An extension of equivariant
bundles by an invariant class is equivariant, so $E$ is, and its
equivariant determinant is that of $V$, which is trivial. The group
$\Gamma$ preserves every factor of $(\PP^1)^4$ and acts trivially on
$H^2(X,\R)$, so $J$ is invariant and induces a K\"ahler class $J_Y$ with
$\pi^*J_Y=J$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:twoblocks}(ii), $E$ is polystable of
slope zero for $J$. For a subsheaf $F\subset E_Y$ the subsheaf
$\pi^*F\subset E$ has $\mu_J(\pi^*F)=4\mu_{J_Y}(F)$, since $\pi$ is \'etale
of degree four, so $E_Y$ is semistable of slope zero. If $F$ is saturated
of slope zero, $\pi^*F$ is a saturated subsheaf of slope zero of the
polystable bundle $E$, hence a direct summand. The projections of $E$ onto
$\pi^*F$ that restrict to the identity form an affine space on which
$\Gamma$ acts, and the average of one of them over $\Gamma$ is an invariant
projection, which descends; so $F$ is a direct summand of $E_Y$, both
summands pull back to direct summands of $E$, and induction on the rank
shows that $E_Y$ is polystable. The Chern character is that of
$V_Y$ because $E$ and $V$ have the same graded pieces. The axionic masses
of Lemma~\ref{lem:branchfactor} are computed from $c_1(E_j)$ in
$H^2(Y,\R)\cong H^2(X,\R)$, so the argument of that lemma applies on $Y$.

(ii) Let $S=\{2,4,5\}$; here $E_S=L_2\oplus L_5\oplus L_4$ is a split
subbundle with $E/E_S\cong L_1\oplus L_3$. By Table~\ref{tab:pieces} the
bundles $L_1$, $L_3$, $L_1L_3$ and $L_aL_1$, $L_aL_3$ for $a\in S$ are
acyclic. The long exact sequences of $0\to E_S\to E\to E/E_S\to0$ and of
the filtration $F^2\subset F^1\subset\Lambda^2E$, whose quotients are
$E_S\otimes(L_1\oplus L_3)$ and $L_1L_3$, give equivariant isomorphisms
$H^\ast(E_S)\cong H^\ast(E)$ and $H^\ast(\Lambda^2E_S)\cong H^\ast(\Lambda^2E)$.
The same holds for $V$ with the same $E_S$, so both sides equal
$H^\ast(L_2)\oplus H^\ast(L_4)\oplus H^\ast(L_5)$ and
$\bigoplus_{a<b\in S}H^\ast(L_aL_b)$ as representations, and the projection
of Section~\ref{sec:fixedbundle} applies verbatim.

(iii) is Theorem~\ref{thm:gammayukawa}(i).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the quotient branch decides]\label{rem:gammascope}
Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedbranch} gives, for the first time in this paper, one
datum on $X_\pi/\Gamma$ satisfying C1 with the qualification of
Section~\ref{sec:fixedbundle}, C2, C3, C4, C5 and C6 at once, and C8 still
fails there, now for every value of the extension moduli.
Corollary~\ref{cor:gammac8} extends the failure of C8 to every $\Gamma$-bundle
with two blocks of size two from the eleven data. Its scope is the
large-volume holomorphic coupling, the cup product, which includes every
order in the singlets that the extension classes represent; worldsheet
instantons and other nonperturbative contributions carry axionic factors
that can violate the torus selection rule, and are not covered. Not
decided here are: the classes $2$ and $7$, which need another free group
of order four; shapes with a block of size three or more, such as the
extension of $L_1\oplus L_3$ by $L_2$ of Section~\ref{sec:pointfailure},
which Section~\ref{sec:filtered} treats; other tetraquadrics, for which the program checks that the bounds of
Theorem~\ref{thm:gammayukawa} also follow from the bound
\eqref{eq:koszulbound} alone; the spectrum on the other shapes of the
fixed datum, where for $L_2\to L_4$, $L_5\to L_3$, $L_1$ the connecting map
$H^1(L_4L_5)\to H^2(L_2L_5)$ may be nonzero; and C7 and C9.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Bundles filtered by the line bundles}\label{sec:filtered}
The two-block bundles are the simplest bundles built from the summands by
extensions. We now treat all such bundles at once, with blocks of any size.
The class $9$ is added to the $\Gamma$-data: its rows have even sum, its
index is $-12$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:twoblocks}(i) excluded it only for two
blocks of size two. On $X_\pi$ its pieces with nonzero cohomology are
$H^1(L_5)$, of dimension $12$ ($n_5=3$), $H^1(L_1L_5)$, of dimension $12$,
and $L_3L_4$, of cohomology $(3,3)$.

\begin{definition}[Filtered $\Gamma$-bundles]\label{def:filtered}
Let $L_1,\dots,L_5$ be the line bundles of the datum \eqref{eq:bundle} or
of one of the classes $6$, $8$, $9$, $10$. A \emph{filtered
$\Gamma$-bundle} is a $\Gamma$-equivariant vector bundle $E$ on $X$ with a
filtration $0=F_0\subset F_1\subset\dots\subset F_5=E$ by $\Gamma$-stable
subbundles such that $F_j/F_{j-1}\cong L_{\sigma(j)}$ equivariantly for a
permutation $\sigma$, where the equivariant structures of the $L_a$ have
characters of product $\mathbf 1$. The permutation $\sigma$ is a
\emph{flag order} of $E$. We write $a\to b$, a \emph{link}, when
$H^1(L_aL_b^{-1})\ne0$.
\end{definition}

A filtered $\Gamma$-bundle has $c_1=0$ and trivial equivariant
determinant, so it descends to $Y$, with the quark and lepton fields of
Section~\ref{sec:gammabranch}. Every bundle of Section~\ref{sec:gammabranch}
is one, with the flag order $s_1,s_2,q_1,q_2,o$; so is every iterated
extension of the $L_a$ and every direct sum of such, with blocks of any
size, such as the extension of $L_1\oplus L_3$ by $L_2$ named in
Remark~\ref{rem:gammascope}. Stability is not assumed. The links are
those of Table~\ref{tab:links}.\footnote{The program
\texttt{code/filtered\_branch.py} of the repository computes the links and
pieces on $X_\pi$ and recomputes the bounds of
Theorem~\ref{thm:filtered} for all $120$ orders and every down-set; its
output is the file \texttt{filtered\_branch\_results.json}.} They are
computed as in Lemma~\ref{lem:pieces}; only whether a group is zero enters
below.

For a flag order $\sigma$ let $P_\sigma$ be the partial order generated by
the links $a\to b$ with $a$ before $b$ in $\sigma$. A set $S$ is a
\emph{down-set} of $P_\sigma$ when it contains every element below one of
its elements.

\begin{lemma}[Exchanging two quotients]\label{lem:exchange}
Let $\sigma$ be a flag order of a filtered $\Gamma$-bundle $E$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)},leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item If $a=\sigma(j-1)$, $b=\sigma(j)$ and $a\to b$ is not a link, the
order obtained by exchanging $a$ and $b$ is a flag order of $E$.
\item Every linear extension of $P_\sigma$ is a flag order of $E$.
\item For every down-set $S$ of $P_\sigma$, $E$ has a $\Gamma$-subbundle
$E_S$ of rank $|S|$, filtered by the $L_a$ with $a\in S$, such that
$E/E_S$ is filtered by the others.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(a) The bundle $F_j/F_{j-2}$ is an extension of $L_b$ by $L_a$ whose class
lies in $H^1(L_aL_b^{-1})=0$. A splitting exists, and its average over
$\Gamma$ is an equivariant splitting, so $F_j/F_{j-2}\cong L_a\oplus L_b$.
Replacing $F_{j-1}$ by the preimage of $L_b$ in $F_j$ gives a filtration
with the two quotients exchanged. (b) Let $\tau$ be a linear extension of
$P_\sigma$, and pass from $\sigma$ to $\tau$ by exchanging adjacent
elements that stand in the order opposite to $\tau$, as in a bubble sort.
Such a pair $x,y$ has never been exchanged before, so $x$ precedes $y$ in
$\sigma$ while $y$ precedes $x$ in $\tau$. If $x\to y$ were a link, then
$x<y$ in $P_\sigma$ and $\tau$ would put $x$ first; so it is not, and (a)
applies at every step. (c) A down-set is the beginning of a linear
extension of $P_\sigma$; take $E_S=F_{|S|}$ for that flag order.
\end{proof}

Parts (b) and (c) of Lemma~\ref{lem:selection}, with their proofs, hold
for such a subbundle $E_S$, with $F^m\Lambda^iE=\operatorname{Im}(\Lambda^mE_S\otimes
\Lambda^{i-m}E)$: a product of classes of levels $l_1,l_2,l_3$ vanishes
when $l_1+l_2+l_3>|S|$, and every class of $H^1(\Lambda^iE)$ has level at
least $m$ when $H^1(L_I)=0$ for every $|I|=i$ with $|I\cap S|<m$. The
proofs used only that $E_S$ and $E/E_S$ are filtered by the line bundles
in and outside $S$. Since $\det E$ and $K_X$ are trivial, $L_I\cong
L_{I^c}^{-1}$ and $H^1(L_I)$ is dual to $H^2(L_{I^c})$ for $|I|=3$; so the
pieces of $H^1(\Lambda^3E)$, which contains the up-type Higgs classes, are
the $L_I$ with $I^c$ a pair $\{a,b\}$ with $H^2(L_aL_b)\ne0$. Since every
$L_a$ and $L_aL_b$ has $H^0=0$, the map $H^1(E_S)\to H^1(E)$ is injective,
and its image in $H^1(E)^\chi$ has dimension $\sum_{a\in S}n_a$.

\begin{theorem}[Filtered bundles]\label{thm:filtered}
Let $E$ be a filtered $\Gamma$-bundle of the datum \eqref{eq:bundle} or of
one of the classes $6$, $8$, $9$, $10$, with any equivariant structures,
let $\rho$ be any Wilson line, and let the Higgs classes be any. Then
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item for the datum \eqref{eq:bundle}: $Y_d=Y_e=0$;
\item for the class $6$: $\rk Y_u\le2$, or $\rk Y_d\le2$ and $\rk Y_e\le2$;
\item for the class $8$: $\rk Y_u\le2$;
\item for the classes $9$ and $10$: $Y_u=Y_d=Y_e=0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\sigma$ be a flag order of $E$. In each case we name a down-set $S$ of
$P_\sigma$, which by Lemma~\ref{lem:exchange}(c) gives a subbundle $E_S$,
and we read the levels off Table~\ref{tab:pieces} and the pieces of the
class $9$ listed above.

(i) No link leaves $L_1$ or $L_3$, so nothing lies above $1$ or $3$ in
$P_\sigma$, and $S=\{2,4,5\}$ is a down-set. Every line bundle and product
with nonzero cohomology, $L_2$, $L_5$, $L_2L_4$, $L_2L_5$, $L_4L_5$, lies
in $S$. So every class of $H^1(E)$ has level at least $1$ and every class
of $H^1(\Lambda^2E)$ level at least $2$, and the down-type and lepton
products, of levels adding up to at least $5>3$, vanish.

(ii) Here $n_3=n_4=n_5=1$, and $L_2L_5$ is the only product with
$H^2\ne0$, so every up-type Higgs class lies in a piece $L_1L_3L_4$. The
only links into $L_3$, $L_4$, $L_5$ are $4\to3$, $5\to4$, $2\to5$. If $3$
is minimal in $P_\sigma$, take $S=\{3\}$. The Higgs piece meets $S$, so
the Higgs classes have level at least $1$, and $Y_u(x,y;h)=0$ when $x$ has
level $1$; these $x$ form the image of $H^1(L_3)^\varepsilon$, of
dimension $1$, so $\rk Y_u\le2$. The same holds with $S=\{4\}$ when $4$ is
minimal. Otherwise $4<3$ and $5<4$ in $P_\sigma$. If $2$ precedes $5$ in
$\sigma$, then $2<5$, nothing lies below $2$ (the links into $L_2$ come
from $L_3$ and $L_5$, which follow $L_2$), and $S=\{2,5\}$ is a down-set.
The pieces $L_1L_2$, $L_1L_5$, $L_2L_4$, $L_2L_5$ of $H^1(\Lambda^2E)$ all
meet $S$, so every class there has level at least $1$, and a product with
a class of $H^1(E)$ of level $1$ vanishes ($1+1+1>2$); these classes form
the image of $H^1(L_5)$, of dimension $1$ in each character, so $\rk
Y_d\le2$ and $\rk Y_e\le2$. If $5$ precedes $2$, nothing lies below $5$,
and $S=\{3,4,5\}$ is a down-set: every class of $H^1(E)$ has level $1$, the
Higgs piece $L_1L_3L_4$ meets $S$ twice, and $1+1+2>3$ gives $Y_u=0$.

(iii) No link enters $L_3$, so $S=\{3\}$ is a down-set. The products with
$H^2\ne0$ are $L_2L_4$, $L_2L_5$, $L_4L_5$, so every Higgs piece of
$\Lambda^3E$ contains $L_3$, and as in (ii) $Y_u(x,y;h)=0$ for $x$ in the
one-dimensional image of $H^1(L_3)^\varepsilon$; hence $\rk Y_u\le2$.

(iv) No link enters $L_5$, and $5\to4$ is the only link into $L_4$, so
$S=\{4,5\}$ is a down-set. All families come from $L_5$, so every class of
$H^1(E)$ has level $1$. The products with nonzero cohomology are
$L_1L_5$, $L_3L_4$ for the class $9$ and $L_1L_5$, $L_2L_4$, $L_2L_5$ for
the class $10$; each meets $S$ in one element, and so do the Higgs pieces
$L_1L_2L_5$ and $L_1L_3L_4$ of $\Lambda^3E$. Every coupling therefore has
levels adding up to at least $3>2$ and vanishes.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[C8 on the filtered branch]\label{cor:filteredc8}
On $Y=X_\pi/\Gamma$, condition $\mathrm{C8}_W$ fails for every bundle that
descends from a filtered $\Gamma$-bundle whose quotients are the line
bundles of one of the eleven data of Table~\ref{tab:eleven} and that has
three families.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The quotients of a $\Gamma$-stable filtration carry equivariant
structures, so every row of the datum has even sum; three families on $Y$
need the index $-12$. Among the eleven data only the five of
Theorem~\ref{thm:filtered} meet both conditions, and for each of them one
of the three Yukawa matrices has rank at most two.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the filtered branch decides]\label{rem:filteredscope}
Corollary~\ref{cor:filteredc8} contains Corollary~\ref{cor:gammac8} and
removes the restriction to blocks of size two: no extension of these line
bundles, of any shape and with any extension classes, gives three Yukawa
matrices of full rank on $X_\pi/\Gamma$. The extension groups do the
work: an extension of $L_b$ by $L_a$ can be undone in the order when
$H^1(L_aL_b^{-1})=0$, and in each datum the line bundles that carry the
families, or the Higgs pieces, can be moved to the beginning of a flag.
The removal of the Abelian factor is not the obstruction: for the datum
\eqref{eq:bundle} the generator $u=(4,-1,-6,4,-1)$ of
Table~\ref{tab:twoblocks} is constant exactly on the sets $\{1,4\}$,
$\{2,5\}$, $\{3\}$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:branchfactor} every block meeting two of the sets
$\{1,4\}$, $\{2,5\}$, $\{3\}$ removes it. The scope is again the
large-volume cup product. The next subsection removes the assumption
that the filtration is $\Gamma$-stable and disposes of the classes $2$
and $7$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Filtrations that are not assumed equivariant}\label{sec:nonequivariant}
A bundle on $Y$ pulls back to a $\Gamma$-equivariant bundle on $X=X_\pi$,
but a filtration of the pullback by line bundles need not be
$\Gamma$-stable, and Definition~\ref{def:filtered} asks that it be. We
show that nothing is lost, and that the two data with an odd row give no
bundle on $Y$ at all.

\begin{lemma}[Indices modulo four]\label{lem:indexmod4}
For $v\in\Z^4$, $\chi(\cO_X(v))\equiv2\pmod 4$ when exactly one
coordinate of $v$ is odd, and $\chi(\cO_X(v))\equiv0\pmod4$ otherwise.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:cover}, $\chi(\cO_X(v))=2(e_3(v)+e_1(v))$, where
$e_1$ and $e_3$ are the elementary symmetric functions of the coordinates,
so its class modulo $4$ is twice the parity of $e_1(v)+e_3(v)$. If $w$
coordinates of $v$ are odd, then $e_1(v)\equiv w$ and
$e_3(v)\equiv\binom w3\pmod2$, and $w+\binom w3$ is odd for $w=1$ and
even for $w=0,2,3,4$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Equivariance comes for free]\label{prop:nonequivariant}
Let $L_1,\dots,L_5$ be the line bundles of one of the eleven data of
Table~\ref{tab:eleven}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item For the classes $2$ and $7$ no vector bundle $F$ on $Y$ has
$\operatorname{ch}(\pi^*F)=\sum_a\operatorname{ch}(L_a)$; in particular
no bundle on $Y$ pulls back to a bundle filtered by their line bundles,
whether or not the filtration is $\Gamma$-stable.
\item For the datum \eqref{eq:bundle} and the classes $6$, $8$, $10$,
$H^0(L_bL_a^{-1})=0$ for $a\ne b$, and every filtration of a
$\Gamma$-equivariant bundle $E$ on $X$ with quotients
$L_{\sigma(1)},\dots,L_{\sigma(5)}$ is $\Gamma$-stable; so $E$ is a
filtered $\Gamma$-bundle with flag order $\sigma$, with some equivariant
structures on the $L_a$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i) If $\sum_ik_i$ is even, $\cO_X(k)$ carries a $\Gamma$-action
(Section~\ref{sec:gammabranch}), so $\cO_X(k)=\pi^*M$ for a line bundle
$M$ on $Y$, and since $\pi$ is \'etale of degree $4$,
$\chi(X,\pi^*F\otimes\cO_X(k))=4\chi(Y,F\otimes M)$ is divisible by $4$.
By Riemann--Roch the left side depends only on
$\operatorname{ch}(\pi^*F)$, so it equals
$\sum_a\chi(L_a\otimes\cO_X(k))$. For the class $2$ take
$k=(1,1,0,0)$: the rows of Table~\ref{tab:eleven} plus $k$ have exactly
one odd coordinate only for $a=5$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:indexmod4} the
sum is $2$ modulo $4$ (it is $-22$). For the class $7$ take
$k=(1,0,1,0)$; again exactly one row plus $k$, the fifth, has one odd
coordinate, and the sum is $-22$.

(ii) The vanishing is read off as in Lemma~\ref{lem:pieces}; the five
line bundles of each of these data are distinct. Let
$0=F_0\subset\dots\subset F_5=E$ be the filtration and
$M=L_{\sigma(1)}=F_1$. From the filtration,
$\dim\operatorname{Hom}(M,E)\le\sum_jh^0(M^{-1}L_{\sigma(j)})=1$, so
$F_1$ is the only subsheaf of $E$ isomorphic to $M$. Each $g\in\Gamma$
preserves the factors of $(\mathbb P^1)^4$, so $g^*M\cong M$, and the
equivariant structure $g^*E\cong E$ carries $g^*F_1$ to a subsheaf of $E$
isomorphic to $M$, that is to $F_1$. Thus $F_1$ is $\Gamma$-stable and
inherits an equivariant structure; $E/F_1$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant and
filtered by $L_{\sigma(2)},\dots,L_{\sigma(5)}$, and induction on the
length gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[C8 for every filtered pullback]\label{cor:nonequivariant}
On $Y=X_\pi/\Gamma$, condition $\mathrm C8_W$ fails for every vector
bundle with three families whose pullback to $X_\pi$ admits a filtration,
$\Gamma$-stable or not, with quotients the line bundles of one of the
eleven data of Table~\ref{tab:eleven}.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Three families need the index $-12$, which leaves the data with $|G|=4$,
and part (i) of Proposition~\ref{prop:nonequivariant} removes the classes
$2$ and $7$. For the datum \eqref{eq:bundle} and the classes $6$, $8$, $10$,
part (ii) makes the filtration $\Gamma$-stable, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:filtered}, which allows any equivariant structures,
applies. For the class $9$, where $L_3=L_4$, the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:filtered}(iv) uses no group action: the splitting in
Lemma~\ref{lem:exchange}(a) exists on $X$, so the flag orders and the
subbundle $E_{\{4,5\}}$ exist for any filtration, and the selection rule
makes every product of the whole groups $H^1(E)$, $H^1(\Lambda^2E)$,
$H^1(\Lambda^3E)$ vanish, hence also its restriction to the invariant
parts. So $Y_u=Y_d=Y_e=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What remains on this quotient]\label{rem:nonequivariantscope}
Part (i) uses only the Chern character, so the classes $2$ and $7$ give no
bundle on $Y$ at all: no deformation of $\bigoplus_aL_a$, stable or not,
descends. For the five $\Gamma$-data, Corollary~\ref{cor:nonequivariant}
covers every bundle on $Y$ whose pullback is built by extensions from the
$L_a$. Not covered are deformations of these bundles whose pullback admits
no such filtration (a Yukawa rank can rise under deformation, since it is
lower semicontinuous), bundles with other Chern characters, other free
quotients and worldsheet instantons.
\end{remark}
